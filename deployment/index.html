    Deployment guide¶

    This page is for an organization that deploys PandoraBox kiosks: the decisions to take first, the keys and the people who hold them, where the kiosks go, the network they need, the rollout, then the daily operation. The technical steps are described in the other pages; this one says which to follow, in which order, and why.

    In short¶

    1. Decide: whose keys, offline or online, kiosk or file transfer station, how many kiosks.
    2. Prepare the keys and name the people who hold them.
    3. Choose the hardware and the places of the kiosks.
    4. Open the network flows of the online kiosks.
    5. Build the image of the organization and test it on a pilot kiosk.
    6. Install the kiosks, then tell the users.
    7. Operate: signatures, image updates, logs, detections.

    Decisions¶

    Whose keys¶

    A kiosk trusts two keys, fixed at its installation (Releases): the Secure Boot key decides what it boots, the update key which signature sets, image updates and maintenance requests it accepts.

    Option Suits Consequences
    Release image of the project Evaluation, a kiosk on the reference hardware Ready to write and verifiable (Verifying a release). No customization: built-in theme, default configuration, no profile online. Its USBGuard rule allows one touch controller, by its serial number and port: another screen unit is blocked. Every signature set and maintenance request must be signed by the update key of the project
    Image of the organization Every production deployment The organization builds its image (Building the image) with its own keys, and chooses its configuration, theme, touchscreen rule, profile and credentials. It keeps its keys and publishes its own signature sets

    In practice, a production deployment builds its own image: it needs its own touchscreen rule, the name of each kiosk, often its theme and the profile online, and an update key it holds itself, to sign its signature sets and maintenance requests. The rest of this page assumes it.

    A kiosk installed with some keys only accepts the updates signed with them. Changing keys means writing a new image to its disk (Initial installation).

    Offline or online¶

    Offline (no profile) Online (--profile online)
    Network None: the image has no network configuration Wired network, DHCP, outgoing flows only (Network)
    Signatures A signature update device, inserted by an operator Downloaded by the kiosk every 6 hours, from the sources or from a set published by the organization
    Image updates A USB key A USB key, or a published update (updates.image_url)
    Logs A maintenance device Sent to a SIEM as they are logged; maintenance device too
    Clock Hardware clock, never corrected NTP
    Credentials in the image None abuse.ch Auth-Key, and the SSH key of a file transfer station: the image must not leave the organization

    An offline kiosk has the smallest attack surface, but its signatures are only as fresh as the last visit of an operator, and its logs only reach the organization when someone brings them back. The kiosk warns on its screen when its signatures are older than 7 days (Signature freshness): plan a visit at least every week, or choose the profile online.

    Kiosk or file transfer station¶

    A kiosk scans the key and removes the dangerous files. A file transfer station also copies the clean files of the verified keys to a server of the organization, and writes on the keys the files that the protected network left for them (Copy to a server). It needs the profile online, a server reachable over SSH, and its credentials in the image.

    Choose a station when the files must enter or leave an isolated network: the users then never plug their keys into a workstation of that network.

    How many kiosks¶

    Each kiosk names itself in its logs and its SIEM messages with kiosk.name (Configuration). It is part of the configuration, hence of the image: without it, every kiosk is called pandora-box, the host name of the image. To tell the kiosks apart in the SIEM and in the maintenance exports, build one image per kiosk, with the same keys and only its name changing. Keep the list of the kiosks: name, place, profile, image version.

    Keys and roles¶

    Role Holds Does
    Key holders (two people) The Secure Boot key and the update key, on two YubiKeys, and their PIN (Release keys) Sign the images, the image updates, the signature sets and the maintenance requests
    Builder The build computer, with Docker and the sources Builds the images and the image updates
    Operators The BIOS supervisor password, the USB keys of maintenance Install the kiosks, bring the signature sets, image updates and maintenance devices, check the kiosks
    Security team The SIEM, the maintenance exports Follows the detections, the refused devices, the kiosks whose signatures are old
    • Create the keys once, on an offline computer, in two YubiKeys (Creating the keys); keep the second one in a safe, apart from the first. A lost Secure Boot key means that every kiosk must be installed again by hand; a lost update key, that they get no more signature sets nor image updates.
    • The development keys that image/build.sh generates (image/mkosi.key, image/update.key) are files on the build computer: never use them for a production kiosk.
    • The BIOS supervisor password protects Secure Boot and the boot order of every kiosk: keep it with the keys, not on the kiosk.
    • The abuse.ch Auth-Key and the SSH key of a station are in the image of an online kiosk: treat the image files and the disks of the kiosks as confidential.

    Hardware¶

    Use the validated hardware: a Lenovo ThinkCentre M720q Tiny and a Waveshare 7-inch touchscreen, in the 3D-printed enclosure (Hardware, Assembly). Another computer or screen needs its own settings: the mode of the screen, the rule of its touchscreen, the disk controller in the initrd. Test it in a pilot first.

    Plan for each kiosk:

    • 2 GB of memory at least (clamd alone uses about 1 GB); with scan.workers = "auto", each file scanned in parallel takes 256 MB more, so 4 GB to scan six files at a time (Configuration);
    • a disk of 32 GB or more: the two system slots (about 2.7 GB), the signature sets (about 1.2 GB each, the previous one kept, and the staging of an online kiosk), the reports, the logs and the quarantine;
    • the USBGuard rule of each touchscreen: it is tied to the serial number and the port of its touch controller. Read it in usb-devices.txt of a maintenance export, or in the usb_device_blocked event of the kiosk, then build the image of that kiosk with it (USB devices);
    • a spare kiosk, installed and with signatures, to swap a failed one.

    Placement¶

    • A place visible to staff, at the entrance of the protected area: a reception desk, a security post, the entrance of a lab. Not a closed room where nobody sees who uses the kiosk.
    • Only the scanning port reachable: the enclosure hides the other ports and the power button. Disable the unused USB ports in the BIOS when it allows it (Initial installation).
    • A power outlet; the kiosk starts again by itself after a power failure (BIOS setting After Power Loss).
    • For an online kiosk, a wired network outlet in a dedicated network segment (VLAN), without access to the internal networks.
    • The user procedure displayed next to the kiosk: see Users.

    Network¶

    An offline kiosk needs nothing. An online kiosk only opens outgoing connections: its firewall drops every incoming connection, and only lets out the services below, each by its own system user (Profile online). Allow the same flows, and only them, from the network segment of the kiosks:

    Flow Destination Protocol, port When
    DHCP DHCP server of the segment UDP 67 always
    DNS DNS servers given by DHCP UDP and TCP 53 always
    NTP NTP servers of the image, else of DHCP, else the Debian pool (Time) UDP 123 always
    ClamAV signatures database.clamav.net HTTPS, TCP 443 updates.sources
    YARA Forge github.com and the GitHub download hosts it redirects to HTTPS, TCP 443 updates.sources
    MalwareBazaar mb-api.abuse.ch HTTPS, TCP 443 updates.sources
    Hashlookup cra.circl.lu HTTPS, TCP 443 updates.sources
    Published signature set, image updates the web server of the organization HTTPS, TCP 443 updates.url, updates.image_url
    Proxy the proxy of the organization its port updates.proxy, in place of the four sources above
    SIEM the SIEM TLS, TCP 6514 by default (TCP or UDP 514) [siem] (SIEM)
    File transfer station the SSH server TCP 22 [airgap] (Copy to a server)

    Two ways to feed the signatures of a fleet:

    • From the sources: every kiosk downloads the sources itself (among them the Hashlookup filter, about 1 GB each month), and needs the abuse.ch Auth-Key in its image.
    • From a published set: a publication computer of the organization runs publish/publish.sh every day and puts the signed set on an internal web server (A published set instead). The kiosks only reach that server, need no credential, and only download the files that changed. Prefer it beyond a few kiosks, or when the kiosks may not reach the Internet.

    Building the images¶

    On the build computer, for each kiosk (see Building the image):

    1. Set the configuration in packaging/pandora-box.toml: kiosk.name, display.language, [updates], [siem], [airgap]. Check it with pandora-box --check-config.
    2. Put the theme of the organization in image/theme/ (Interface), the certificate authorities of the SIEM in image/siem-ca.pem, the credentials in image/credentials.toml, the touchscreen rule in image/mkosi.extra/etc/usbguard/rules.conf.
    3. Build with the keys of the organization, with or without --profile online.
    4. Keep the configuration of each kiosk outside the image (a private repository, for instance): the next image of that kiosk is built from it.

    The image version is the time of the build: every image update must be newer than the installed one (System updates).

    Pilot¶

    Before the fleet, run the image in a virtual machine (Testing with QEMU), then install a pilot kiosk and keep it a few weeks in a real place:

    • the touchscreen confirms a cleaning;
    • the EICAR test file is reported and removed;
    • keys of every filesystem used in the organization (vfat, exFAT, NTFS, ext4) are scanned; an encrypted key or a phone is refused, as expected;
    • a signature update device, an image update and a maintenance device are accepted;
    • for an online kiosk: the signatures are updated by themselves, the SIEM receives the events, the clock is right;
    • for a station: the clean files reach the server, the outbound files reach the key;
    • the scan time of the usual keys of the users is acceptable (Hardware).

    Installing the kiosks¶

    For each kiosk (Initial installation):

    1. Configure the BIOS: supervisor password, Secure Boot in setup mode, boot from the internal disk only, VT-d on, power on after a power failure.
    2. Write the image of the kiosk to its disk.
    3. At the first boot, check that Secure Boot is in User mode.
    4. Insert a first signature set: until then, the kiosk shows NO VALID SIGNATURES and scans nothing.
    5. Check it with the EICAR test file and a maintenance device: failed-services.txt is empty, signatures.txt shows the set.
    6. Write its name, place, image version and the date in the list of the kiosks.

    Users¶

    Tell the users what the kiosk does, and what it does not:

    • When: every key that comes from outside, or was used outside, is scanned before it is plugged into a workstation of the organization.
    • How: insert the key, wait for the result, touch the screen to remove the dangerous files, remove the key. The posters of docs/poster/ show these steps, to print and display next to the kiosk.
    • The result: a key not verified must not be used: a file could not be scanned (encrypted archive, file too big, error). The user brings it to the security team, or does without it.
    • The limits: a clean result means that no engine detected anything, not that the key is safe (Threat model). Workstations keep their own protections.
    • Who to call: the contact of the security team, in the theme of the kiosk (Texts) or on the poster.

    Operation¶

    Recurring tasks¶

    Task Frequency How
    Signature set every day (online), at least every week (offline) Signatures: automatic online; a signature update device otherwise
    Image update every month, and for each security update of Debian that concerns the kiosk rebuild with the same keys and configuration, then System updates
    Logs continuously (SIEM), or at each visit (maintenance device) Logs, SIEM
    Check of the kiosk at each visit the screen shows no signature warning; the enclosure is closed and intact; no device other than the kiosk is plugged in
    Quarantine and reports every year see Data on the kiosk
    YubiKeys every year both YubiKeys still sign (a test signature); the PINs are known by the key holders

    What to watch¶

    In the SIEM, or in the maintenance exports (Useful events, Dashboards):

    Event Action
    infected_files, device_verdict malicious Find out where the key comes from, and the workstations it was plugged into before the kiosk
    usb_device_blocked with composite A key that is also a keyboard: keep the key, it is an attack tool
    usb_device_blocked, other devices Someone plugged in a keyboard, a network adapter or a phone: check the kiosk
    device_suspicious_behavior A key that disconnected itself during its scan: keep it for analysis
    signatures_refused, maintenance_refused A device signed by another key, too old or modified: who brought it?
    No signatures_changed for several days (online) The kiosk no longer reaches its sources: network, proxy, Auth-Key
    No event at all from a kiosk Turned off, unplugged, or failing to boot: go and see

    Detections¶

    When a kiosk reports a malicious file:

    1. The file is copied to the quarantine of the kiosk, then removed from the key after a touch of the screen. Its SHA-256 and the engines that detected it are in the scan report and in the infected_files event.
    2. Ask the user where the key comes from, and on which computers it was used: they may be infected.
    3. A copy of the file is in the quarantine of the kiosk (/var/lib/pandora-box/quarantine/), on its disk only: the maintenance export does not bring it back. To analyze it, take the disk out, in a lab.

    Failures¶

    Symptom Cause, action
    NO VALID SIGNATURES No set installed, or the installed set fails its verification: insert a signature update device
    Signature warning on the screen The signatures are older than 7 days: insert a set, or check the network of an online kiosk
    The touchscreen does not confirm USBGuard blocks it: another unit or another port than the rule of the image (USB devices)
    The kiosk restarts on the previous version The new image failed to boot 3 times: boots.txt of a maintenance export (If the new version fails)
    The kiosk does not start Take the spare kiosk; check the BIOS settings and the disk of the failed one

    Data on the kiosk¶

    The data partition (/var) of a kiosk holds the scan reports (names and hashes of the files of the users), the logs and the quarantine (copies of malware). It is not encrypted: whoever takes the disk can read it (Threat model).

    • Decide how long the reports and the quarantine are kept, and record the kiosk in the register of processing of the organization when the reports name people's files. The logs are rotated every month and kept 12 months (Logs).
    • A file transfer station keeps neither reports nor quarantine on its disk (A stolen kiosk).
    • A kiosk is a place where malware is kept: do not let it leave the organization without wiping its disk.

    Decommissioning¶

    1. Bring back the logs with a maintenance device, if they are still needed.
    2. Wipe the disk (blkdiscard, or a full overwrite of a hard disk): it holds the quarantine, the reports, and the credentials of an online kiosk.
    3. In the BIOS: Security → Secure Boot → Restore Factory Keys, and remove the supervisor password, before giving the computer to another use.
    4. For an online kiosk or a station: revoke its credentials (abuse.ch Auth-Key, SSH key on the server) when they are not shared by the other kiosks, and close its network flows.
    5. Remove it from the list of the kiosks.
